# Urinalysis Reference Guidelines

Specific gravity in a urinalysis measures urine concentration, with normal ranges from 1.005 to 1.030. High specific gravity indicates concentrated urine, often due to dehydration. Low specific gravity suggests dilute urine, which can happen with excessive fluid intake or conditions affecting the kidneys' ability to concentrate urine.

The pH of urine indicates its acidity or alkalinity, normally ranging from 4.6 to 8.0. An acidic pH can be influenced by diet (like high protein) or indicate conditions like ketoacidosis. An alkaline pH may result from vegetarian diets, urinary tract infections, or certain kidney issues.

Protein in the urine (proteinuria) is often a sign of kidney disease. Normal urine contains little to no protein. Elevated protein levels can be caused by chronic conditions like diabetes or high blood pressure, or temporary conditions like intense exercise or fever.

Glucose is typically not found in urine. Its presence (glycosuria) usually indicates that blood sugar levels are exceptionally high, most commonly due to uncontrolled diabetes mellitus.

Ketones in the urine suggest that the body is burning fat for energy instead of carbohydrates. This can occur in uncontrolled diabetes (diabetic ketoacidosis), fasting, or severe carbohydrate-restricted diets.

Bilirubin is a product of red blood cell breakdown. Its presence in urine can be an early indicator of liver disease or bile duct obstruction, as normal urine does not contain bilirubin.

Leukocyte esterase is an enzyme produced by white blood cells. A positive result in urinalysis typically indicates inflammation or infection in the urinary tract or kidneys.

Nitrites in urine are strongly suggestive of a bacterial urinary tract infection. Normal urine is devoid of nitrites, as they are produced when bacteria convert dietary nitrates into nitrites.
